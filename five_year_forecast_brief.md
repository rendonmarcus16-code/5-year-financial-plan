# Five-Year Financial Forecast & Variance Dashboard — Project Brief

**To:** Budget & Management Analyst
**From:** Budget Manager
**Re:** FY2026 Forecast Preparation & Quarterly Variance Reporting

## Background

Attached are five fiscal years (FY2021–FY2025) of monthly budget data:

- `revenue_by_source.csv` — General Fund revenue by source (Property Tax, Sales
  Tax, Fees & Permits, Fines & Forfeitures, Intergovernmental Revenue, Other
  Revenue), budgeted vs. actual, by month
- `expenditure_by_department.csv` — Departmental expenditures (Police, Fire,
  Public Works, Parks & Recreation, Library, Health, Animal Care Services,
  Municipal Court), budgeted vs. actual, by month

The City's fiscal year runs October 1 – September 30 (e.g., FY2025 = Oct 2024
through Sep 2025).

## Assignment

Build a Power BI dashboard that could realistically support the Budget
Office's core responsibilities:

1. **Five-Year Trend & Forecast**
   Show revenue and expenditure trends across all five fiscal years, and
   project FY2026 based on historical trend. (Power BI's built-in forecasting
   in a line chart, or a simple trend calculation, both work — pick whichever
   you can explain clearly.)

2. **Budget vs. Actual Variance**
   For both revenue and expenditure, show variance (actual − budgeted) by
   category/department and by year. Highlight where actuals are
   consistently running ahead of or behind budget — that pattern matters
   more than any single month's number.

3. **Quarterly / Annual Rollup View**
   Since the real role prepares quarterly status reports for the City
   Manager and City Council, include a view that rolls the monthly data up
   to quarterly or annual totals — not just raw monthly figures.

4. **Executive Summary**
   A short written or visual summary (a text box, a card, or a dashboard
   page) stating: which revenue sources are outperforming or underperforming
   budget, which departments are running over budget and by how much, and
   what the FY2026 forecast suggests the City should watch for.

## Deliverable

A `.pbix` file (or exported screenshots if sharing the file isn't practical)
showing the above, plus a short written narrative of your top 2–3 findings —
the same way you did for the vendor audit report.

## Note

This dataset is fully synthetic, built to resemble a realistic municipal
General Fund budget — it is not real City of San Antonio financial data.
